2026-11-03 — Old languages: Spain, Frisia, Armenia and the Book of Rites
  • Word Map: 84 more words in historical languages. The Old Spanish bat is murciego, the "blind mouse", attested about 1250; Old Frisian thunder is thuner, and Old Spanish and Old Frisian, like Latin, have only the general word for "brother". Classical Armenian thunder is որոտ and its whale կէտ, borrowed from Greek κῆτος. Middle Irish Monday is lúan, the Moon. The Greeks are यवनाः Yavanas in Sanskrit, from Old Persian Yauna, "Ionian". The jellyfish is the "sea lung" in both Latin, pulmō marīnus, and Greek, πλεύμων, and the Middle Chinese hiccup is 噦, which the Book of Rites forbids in front of one's parents. The Nahuatl little finger is mahpilxōcoyōtl, the "youngest child" finger, and the Syriac one ܚܨܪܐ comes from a root "to narrow". Gothic's only word for grandmother, 𐌰𐍅𐍉, is used once, for Timothy's grandmother Lois. Ottoman Turkish has آغا ağa for an older brother, and the Old Turkic inscriptions call China 𐱃𐰉𐰍𐰲 Tabgač, after the Tuoba of Northern Wei. Syriac adds China ܨܝܢ and Coptic India ϩⲉⲛⲧⲟⲩ. The Aztec Milky Way is citlālīcue, "her skirt of stars", which Molina's 1555 dictionary glosses as the Camino de Santiago; the Syriac shooting star ܢܝܙܟܐ is also a "spear"; and sugar is śakkār in Tocharian B but gula, from Sanskrit guḍa, in Old Javanese. In Ugarit thunder is 𐎅𐎄 Haddu, the storm god himself; the Aramaic and Hebrew whale is the תנין, the sea monster that the King James Bible calls the "great whales" of Genesis 1:21; Classical Persian has the bat شب‌پره, the "night-wing", and the Pali name for India is जम्बुदीप, the "rose-apple continent". The Old Irish butterfly etelachán is the "little flying creature", the Old East Slavic little finger мѣзиньць was the "youngest son", Classical Armenian Monday երկշաբաթի is the "second of the week", and Old Frisian morgen means both morning and tomorrow. From Gerhard Köbler's dictionaries: the Old Saxon bat fletharmūs, the "flutter-mouse", found once in an 11th-century glossary; the Old Frisian rainbow reinboga; and the Old Frisian little finger slūtere, the "locker" that closes the fist. The Old and Middle High German little finger is ōrfingar, ōrvinger, the "ear finger", like Latin auriculāris. Sanskrit's little finger कनिष्ठिका is "the smallest" and its thunder स्तनयित्नु, personified in the plural as the children of Lightning, goes back to the Rigveda (Monier-Williams); the Pali bat वग्गुलि is the "flutterer", and Pali जेट्ठ, the first-born, is the elder brother (Pali Text Society dictionary). From the Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon: the Syriac thumb ܟܪܬܐ and hiccup ܦܘܩܬܐ, and the Aramaic thumb ܐܠܝܘܢܐ of Targum Onkelos, little finger ܚܨܪܐ, silk ܫܐܪܝܐ and Monday ܬܪܝܢ ܒܫܒܐ, "two in the week".
2026-11-02 — More words in the old languages, continued
  • Word Map: 26 more words in historical languages. Middle Korean adds the whale 고래, the rainbow ·므지·게, the "water door", the thunder ·울에, from a verb "to roar", and the bat :ᄇᆞᆰ·쥐, whose second half is 쥐 "rat". The Old Japanese thunder いかづち is the "awesome spirit", the Syriac Milky Way is the "path of chaff", as in Arabic and Turkish, and Gothic's only word for tomorrow, found once in Matthew 6:30, is related to English yester-. Classical Arabic adds the bat خفاش, the "day-blind one", the little finger خنصر, the grandmother جدة, and شهاب, the Quran's blazing flame, for the shooting star, along with sugar سكر and rice أرز. Old English names Greece Grēcland, "the Greeks' land", Middle English has Grece and Inde, and Old French adds thunder tuneire and blue bleu. Monday is the moon's day in Old High German mānotag, Middle High German māntac and Old Irish lúan, but in Ottoman Turkish it is بازار ایرتسی, "the day after market day". Old East Slavic Китаи, the ancestor of Russian Китай, is named after the Khitan, and Old and Middle English call Germany Germania and Germanie.
