  • Words that were real words but meant something else. In Runyankore, Kiga and Soga, “fish” was the word for the lake. In Sudanese Arabic, “nose” was the word for mouth. In Mường, “book” was the word for iron. In Shan, “tooth” was the word for green, which differs from it only by a tone mark. And in Chittagonian, “love” was the word for girl.
  • A new round of filling in the Word Map, this time in the 266 large languages that still had gaps — mostly in Africa: Bukusu gets snow, mountain and wheel; Nuer rain, horse and salt; Hazaragi thirteen words at once.
  • Most come from Bible translations, checked against a control verse. A “snow” is only accepted when the same Bible uses a different word for hail; several languages turned out to use one word for both, and those cells stay empty rather than wrong.
