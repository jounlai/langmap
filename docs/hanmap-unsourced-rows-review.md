# Review of ten unsourced Han Map rows

Reviewed 2026-10-02. “None known” means that I found research on the variety or feature, but no published source supporting a stable character-by-character reading list for most of the Han Map’s 104 characters.

## Final editorial decision

The final decision below uses Han Map's stated purpose: comparing a representative reading for each character. A categorical, explicitly conditioned phonological derivation is acceptable if generated cells are identified as modeled. A frequency distribution of learner errors or highly individual heritage-speaker realizations is not converted into a single representative reading.

| Action | Rows | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Rebuild from lexical/survey data** | `ko_bus`, `zh_zz` | Transcribe the source entries and preserve reported variants. |
| **Rebuild as a rule-derived model** | `ja_kgs`, `ja_thk`, `ko_zai` | Narrow locality/cohort, publish the derivation rules, and mark every generated cell `modeled`, not `attested`. |
| **Rebuild and rename from a character lexicon** | `zh_song` | Replace it with “Late Middle Chinese (Pulleyblank 1991)” if that reconstruction is not already represented. |
| **Remove now; reconsider after obtaining the full source tables** | `ja_okn` | The present `/e→i, o→u/` forms are unsupported. Zayasu's full Common-Japanese correspondence tables are needed before rebuilding a defined southern-central Okinawa cohort. |
| **Remove from the character-reading map** | `zh_us`, `zh_th`, `yue_us` | Retain the research in editorial notes or a future sociophonetic view; do not turn variable cohort behavior or error rates into categorical character readings. |

For `zh_th`, the 2017 Thai-Chinese learner study is strong enough to support a separate visualization of substitution frequencies, but its finding that most participants retain all four Mandarin tones directly contradicts the current row. For `zh_us` and `yue_us`, the best studies likewise describe retained contrasts, partial mergers, developmental effects, and large individual differences. These are valuable sociophonetic results, but they do not define new lexical readings for 104 isolated characters.

## Notes

### Decision summary

- **Rebuild from lexical or survey data:** `ko_bus`, `zh_zz`.
- **Rebuild from explicitly documented phonological rules after narrowing the row:** `ja_kgs`, `ja_thk`.
- **Potential rule-based rebuild pending the full correspondence tables:** `ja_okn`.
- **Rebuild and rename from a character lexicon if it is not duplicative:** `zh_song` → Late Middle Chinese (Pulleyblank 1991).
- **Rebuild from a documented but observational rule set:** `ko_zai`.
- **Rebuild only if cells support weighted variants:** `zh_th`.
- **Replace with a named experimental/corpus profile, or remove:** `zh_us`, `yue_us`.
- **Keep as is:** none. Every row presently lacks provenance for its cells, even where the underlying variety and some sample forms are plausible.

### Busan accent representation

Ito’s notation should be preserved rather than flattened. H(H) and H(L) are neutralized in isolation but contrast before an enclitic; L(H) is realized as a long rise in isolation. A useful Han Map cell would therefore contain the lexical class and a display pronunciation, with a note that the class may require a following diagnostic suffix. The source also makes speaker disagreement visible, which is evidence to retain variants rather than silently choosing one form.

Ito’s earlier “The Sino-Korean Accentuation in Middle Korean” (1999), [*Gengo Kenkyu* 116, 97–125](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/gengo1939/1999/116/1999_116_97/_article), is valuable historical background, but it does not turn the modern Busan classes into an exceptionless one-to-one reflex of Chinese tone.

### Zhengzhou rebuilding method

Use one edition and transcription system throughout. The 1998 audio-archive volume is preferable because its 764-character list exceeds the map’s needs and can be checked against recordings. Record the consultant metadata and year: treating a single late-twentieth-century city survey as timeless “Zhengzhou Mandarin” would conceal real change. Lu’s 1992 dialect description can be a cross-check, but values should not be silently combined when the two sources differ.

The national digitization project confirms that the original *Xiandai Hanyu Fangyan Yinku* material includes Zhengzhou and character recordings; see the [National Office for Philosophy and Social Sciences project report](https://www.nopss.gov.cn/n1/2019/1216/c418022-31507981.html).

### Historical-row scope

“Song–Ming Classical” should not be repaired by blending several reconstructions. *Zhongyuan Yinyun* is a specific 1324 Yuan source and already has its own row. Pulleyblank’s Late Middle Chinese character lexicon offers a coherent replacement if that reconstruction is absent from the map. Any other addition, such as an explicitly sourced Song rhyme-table reconstruction or *Hongwu Zhengyun*, should receive its own date, source, reconstruction author, and conventions.

### Evidence threshold for heritage and regional rows

A paper establishing that a feature occurs somewhere in a population does not by itself establish a citation form for every character. A replacement row needs either (a) a dictionary/survey containing the characters, (b) a reproducible corpus extraction with named speakers, locality, elicitation context, and variants, or (c) explicit phonological rules whose input, environment, ordering, locality, and exceptions are adequately specified. Case (c) should be labeled as rule-derived in the data so users can distinguish generated forms from directly attested ones.
