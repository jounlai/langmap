Why is 十回 in Japanese read "jikkai" and not "jūkai"?

Because 十 used to end in -p. Middle Chinese had it; Japanese borrowed it as jifu, which became jū — except in compounds, where the old consonant survives as the little pause of the sokuon.

Korean still says sip. Cantonese sap. Vietnamese thập.

Japanese kept the fossil in one place only: the gemination.
