The Sinosphere cuts the mouth in two.

牙 is the back tooth and the tusk. 齒 is the front tooth. Every Chinese variety uses 牙 for an ordinary tooth.

But the neighbours did not take the frame. Japanese says 歯 ha, Korean 이 i, Vietnamese răng — and none of them is borrowed from Chinese. They kept their own words.

The script was shared. The way of dividing up the mouth was not.

Meanwhile Indo-European named the thing from what it does. Proto-Indo-European *h₃dónts is the present participle of *h₁ed-, "to eat". A tooth is, grammatically, "the one that is eating".

One family splits the mouth by position. Another names it by function. Same organ, entirely different handles.
