Everyone knows tea splits into cha and te. There is a third answer.

Almost every language's word for tea descends from one of two routes out of China. The overland Silk Road carried northern Chinese chá — chai, çay. Dutch ships carried the Min Chinese tê by sea — tea, thé, Tee.

Mapped, it cuts Eurasia cleanly in two.

Except for a couple of languages that belong to neither. Polish herbata and Lithuanian arbata preserve a rare third root, derived from Latin.

When a rule divides the world this neatly, it is tempting to assume it covers everything. Real maps usually have a third answer tucked into them somewhere.

For the most-drunk beverage on earth after water, that seems about right.
