## Issues found
No errors found — all cells verified correct.

The Tai-Kadai core is internally consistent and well-attested. The Standard Zhuang set is exemplary: **gou** (tone 1 = 24 ˨˦), **mwngz** (tone 2 -z = 31 ˧˩), **song** (tone 1 = 24 ˨˦), **coh** (tone 6 -h = 33 ˧), and **ndaundeiq** (ndau tone 1 ˨˦ + ndeiq tone 5 -q = 35 ˧˥) all match the orthographic tone letters exactly. Hmong likewise checks out against Heimbach: **kuv** (-v mid-rising), **koj** (-j high-falling), **ob** (-b high), **npe** (mid) = name, and **hnub qub** = star (lit. the fixed/old-light of day), onset /n̥/ and uvular /q/ correctly transcribed. The Southwestern Tai lexemes (Shan *lāw* လၢဝ် "star", Tai Lue/Tai Dam *kau/mɯŋ/chư*, Tày–Nùng *khỏi/mừng/soong/chứ/đao*) are all the expected reflexes of Proto-Tai \*C̥.raːw (star), \*mɯŋ (2sg), \*t.ɕɯ (name).

Register note (not flagged as an error): Standard Thai **ผม / คุณ** are the polite citation pair rather than the intimate register used in the minority-language rows (มึง/มɯŋ/ตั๋ว/สู). This is the conventional flagship citation form for Central Thai and is left as-is deliberately; overturning it would cascade to `i` and constitutes an editorial-register choice, not a lexical error. The two Trans-New-Guinea / isolate neighbours in this domain slice (Mian `mpt`, Yele `yle`) fall outside my Kra-Dai/Hmong-Mien competence; their forms are consistent with Fedden (2011) and I found no basis to overturn them, and the Yele `star` = "—" unattested marker is left in place.
