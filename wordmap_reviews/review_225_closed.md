I am Dr. Elena Marroquín-Voss, a descriptive linguist specializing in emerging and village sign languages, with fieldwork focus on Idioma de Señas de Nicaragua (ISN / Nicaraguan Sign Language). My primary references are Kegl, Senghas & Coppola (1999) "Creation through contact: Sign language emergence and sign language change in Nicaragua" (in DeGraff, ed., *Language Creation and Language Change*), Senghas & Coppola (2001) "Children creating language" (*Psychological Science*), and the Nicaraguan deaf-community documentation compiled by the Asociación Nacional de Sordos de Nicaragua (ANSNIC), including the *Diccionario del Idioma de Señas de Nicaragua* (ANSNIC, 1997). For cross-linguistic sign structure I rely on Sandler & Lillo-Martin (2006) *Sign Language and Linguistics of Sign Languages*. A methodological note governs this review: sign languages are visual-gestural and lack any conventional native written orthography, and their manual/non-manual articulation is not representable in the phonemic-segmental broad-IPA convention used by this corpus. The corpus therefore deliberately marks such cells "—" (unattested in the orthography+IPA representation), which is the linguistically correct treatment for ISN.
