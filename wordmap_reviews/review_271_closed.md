# Wordmap review #271 — Tibeto-Burman (part 2) (5 new words: i, you, two, name, star)

## Reviewer self-introduction (ペルソナ自己紹介)
I am Dr. Rangkhu Marak-Weidert, a descriptive linguist working on Bodo-Garo, Loloish, Karenic and the Sino-Tibetan periphery, with side competence in Batanic (Philippine-type) Austronesian. For this batch I lean on Robbins Burling's *The Language of the Modhupur Mandi (Garo)* (2004) and his *Garo–English Dictionary* for grt; on David Bradley's and Inga-Lill Hansson's Akha materials (Hansson, *Akha–English–Thai Dictionary*) plus Paul Lewis's Akha lexicon for ahk; on Kurabe/Hanson's *A Dictionary of the Kachin Language* and Dai Qingxia's Jingpho work for kac; on Kato Atsuhiko's and Audra Phillips's Pwo Karen grammars/lexica for kjp; on Chen Kang & Tian Desheng's Tujia (土家语) descriptions and the *土家语简志* for tji; and on D.V. Rau & M. Dong's *Yami Dictionary* (2006) together with Ferrell's Batanic comparative notes for tao. I cross-checked pronoun sense (free vs. oblique/possessive), numeral vs. ordinal, and tone-mark plausibility for each Loloish/Karenic/Tujia cell.

## Issues found
No errors found — all cells verified correct.

The Yami free pronouns yaken (1sg) / imo (2sg), dóa "two", ngaran "name" and vituen "star" match Rau & Dong. The Jingpho set ngai / nang / lahkawng / mying / shagan is standard Hanson orthography with correct forms. Garo anga / nang'a / gni / bimung / aski are all attested (nang'a is the valid A·chik-dialect nominative alongside Modhupur na'a, so not an error). Akha nga / naw / nyi / myeh / aqgeu and Pwo Karen ya / na / ni / mi / sha carry sense-appropriate, tonally plausible values. Tujia nga / ni / nie / mingzi / sei are correct; mingzi is the ordinary (Sinitic-derived but fully nativised) word for "name" actually used by speakers and denotes the appellation sense, so it is not flaggable.
