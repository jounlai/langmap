The cuneiform/logographic mix for Elamite (native *hiš* "name" written syllabically 𒄭𒅖; Sumerograms MIN/MUL for the numeral and *star*, whose native Elamite forms are unattested) is a defensible representational choice, not an error. Etruscan's `mi` "I" and `zal` (/tsal/) "two" are correct, and the three `—` cells (2sg, name, star) are legitimate unattested markers for the Tyrsenian record. Iberian's lone `bi` "two" matches the Orduña–Ferrer numeral reconstruction; its other `—` markers are correct. Pirahã's `hoí` (falling-then-rising, glossed "two") correctly encodes Everett's well-known relative-quantity term rather than a fixed cardinal, and the two-tone marking throughout (*tì, gíxai, íga, apapaí*) is internally consistent. The remaining fragmentary forms (Nivkh, Huave, Páez, Hadza, Sandawe, Warao, Mochica, Yuchi, Kusunda, Rotokas) were checked against the available wordlists; several sit at the edge of what the primary sources securely gloss, but I could not establish any of them as an outright error with a confidently attested replacement, so per round-3 discipline I leave them rather than risk introducing a false "correction" into the live data.
