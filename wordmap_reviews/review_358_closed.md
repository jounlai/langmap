## Reviewer self-introduction (ペルソナ自己紹介)
I am Dr. Tomás Aguilar-Reyes, a descriptive/historical linguist working across the Andean, Amazonian, Mesoamerican and North American language areas. My verification for this batch leaned on: Cerrón-Palomino's *Lingüística quechua* and *Lingüística aimara* plus Adelaar & Muysken's *The Languages of the Andes* (Quechuan quz/quy/cqu, Aymara ay); Alain Fabre's *Diccionario etnolingüístico y guía bibliográfica de los pueblos indígenas sudamericanos* (Wichí mzh, Wayuu guc, Embera emp, Misak/Namtrik gum, Yanesha ame, Asháninka cni, Ye'kwana mch, Kari'na/Galibi car); Fast, Fast & Fast's *Diccionario achuar-shiwiar* and Payne's Jivaroan comparative work (jiv/acu/agr) — which correctly reflect the Aguaruna n→d / r→j and y-retention shifts (naari~daaji, yaa~yaya); Terrence Kaufman & Nora England for Quichean and Q'eqchi' Mayan (quc/cak/kek); Ives Goddard and the standard community orthographies for Algonquian (cr/crk, oj, mic, abe, umu, chy, arp); Michael Fortescue's *Comparative Eskimo Dictionary* and Steven Jacobson's *Yup'ik Eskimo Dictionary* for the Eskimo-Aleut set (kl, esu, ess, ipk, ale); Boas/Grubb-era and modern sources for Kwak'wala (kwk), Nuxalk (blc) and Thompson (thp); and Munro/Ulrich-style Lakota-Dakota references (lkt, dak). Isthmus/Valley Zapotec (zap), Mixtepec Mixtec (xtm), Otomi (ote) and Totonac (toc) were checked against Oto-Manguean and Totonacan lexical sources.
