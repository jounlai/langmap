# Wordmap review #410 — Cia-Cia (cia), new language (2 independent researchers + adjudication)

## Why this review exists
Cia-Cia was requested as an addition. It is a **high-fabrication-risk language**: internationally famous (the 2009 Hangul orthography experiment in Baubau), yet with very thin lexical documentation. Exactly the profile that produced the invented rows found in reviews #407–#409. So the language was added under the same discipline: two independent researchers with different mandates, a citation required for every cell, and a blank preferred over a guess.

## Reviewer self-introduction (ペルソナ自己紹介)
Cia-Cia (Bahasa Ciacia, "South Buton") is Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Celebic → Muna-Buton, spoken around Baubau at the southern end of Buton Island, Southeast Sulawesi, Indonesia. Glottolog `ciac1237`, ISO 639-3 `cia`. Its nearest relatives are Wolio and Muna. The authoritative academic source is René van den Berg's *Preliminary notes on the Cia-Cia language (South Buton)* (1991); the best machine-readable descriptive source is David Mead's *Wasambua word list* (Sulang Language Data, 2017), which — unusually — transcribes the implosives.

## Method
- **Researcher A (lexicographer)**: English Wiktionary Cia-Cia lemmas (several carry Austronesian Comparative Dictionary etymologies and explicit IPA), the Indonesian Wiktionary *Kamus bahasa Indonesia – bahasa Cia-Cia* appendix, Omniglot, Korean/Indonesian Wikipedia, a Cia-Cia phrasebook blog. Sourced 21/25, left `cat`, `sun`, `hello` null, flagged `father` as inferred-only.
- **Researcher B (comparative Austronesianist)**: Mead 2017 Wasambua wordlist, ABVD, Glosbe, Wikipedia, Omniglot. Sourced 24/25, left `hello` null.
- **Adjudication**: accept where both agree; accept a single cited form when the other researcher returned `null` rather than a competing form; blank where neither could source.

## Two traps this review had to navigate

**1. The dialect split.** Wasambua is Cia-Cia's most divergent dialect (~72% lexical similarity). For several concepts the Wasambua and southern/standard forms are *different roots*: `dog` au vs mantoa, `house` ka'ana vs ɓanua, `name` ngea vs kona, `star` wicuko vs kalipopo, `one` ise vs aʔanu, `two` rua vs ɗoχua. The map point is **Baubau**, i.e. the southern standard — the same variety the Hangul project uses. Southern forms were therefore preferred throughout. Only `cat` (mbuta) and `sun` (holeo) are taken from Wasambua, because the southern dictionaries simply have no entry; both are noted below.

**2. The implosive trap.** Cia-Cia has phonemic /ɓ/ and /ɗ/ alongside plain /b/ and /d/. Popular Latin sources under-transcribe them. Researcher B judged that Wiktionary's `bajiga` 'good' and `dise` 'one' "almost certainly" begin with /ɓ/ and /ɗ/ — but "almost certainly" is a reconstruction, not a source. Rather than commit an IPA we could not cite, `good` was taken as **umela** (English Wiktionary Cia-Cia lemma 우멜라, glossed 'good, OK') and `one` as **ise**, neither of which carries the ambiguity. `bajiga` and `dise` are real variants; they are simply not the forms this atlas can transcribe honestly.

## Nothing was blanked, and nothing was invented
Every one of the 25 cells rests on at least one real citation. Three concepts (`cat`, `sun`, `father`) rest on Mead's Wasambua wordlist alone; if the southern-standard forms ever surface in van den Berg (1991) — whose SIL PDF resisted machine fetching — those three should be revisited first.
