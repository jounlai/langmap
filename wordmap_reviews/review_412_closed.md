# Wordmap review #412 — Gondi (gon): the row was Hindi

## Why this review exists
The round-2 rally (#411) found that **16 of Gondi's 25 cells held Hindi words, transcribed with Hindi IPA**. It refused to correct them, because inventing Dravidian-looking replacements for a poorly-served language is worse than leaving a known-bad cell. This review rebuilt the row from cited sources.

## Reviewer self-introduction (ペルソナ自己紹介)
Gondi (Gōṇḍī, ISO 639-3 `gon`, Glottolog `gond1265`) is a South-Central Dravidian language of central India — 2.7–3 million L1 speakers across Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra and Telangana, written in Devanagari in the north and Telugu script in the south. It is a **dialect continuum** (Adilabad, Betul, Chhindwara, Koya, Maria, Muria, Sironcha…) and it is **heavily contact-affected**, borrowing freely from Hindi, Marathi and Urdu. Those two facts make it unusually easy to get wrong, and unusually easy to *look* right while being wrong.

## The defect
The row's surface used a dual `Devanagari / Telugu` form, which read as "the same Gondi word in the two scripts Gondi is written in." It was not. It was **a Hindi word paired with its Telugu translation** — neither of them Gondi:

## Method
The 12 unsourced concepts went to a researcher required to cite every form and to return `null` rather than reconstruct one. It sourced **all 12**, from Subrahmanyam's *A Descriptive Grammar of Gondi* (1968), Burrow & Emeneau's DEDR, DravLex (Kolipakam et al. 2018), Trench (1919), Mitchell's *Maria Gondi* (1942), Wiktionary Gondi lemmas, and the Glosbe/SIL Adilabad Gondi material.

It also reported two near-misses it caught itself on, which is the reason to trust the rest: a DEDR page-fetch produced "immaṭ/mīṭ" as Gondi *water* — debunked, DEDR 3690 has **no Gondi reflex** of Proto-Dravidian \*nīr — and Wiktionary's `नल्ला nallā` was nearly taken as 'good' until its gloss turned out to be **'black'**.

## Honest caveats
- **`father` and `good` are medium confidence.** Subrahmanyam gives both *tappe* and *bābo* for father; *tappe* is the more distinctively Dravidian, *bābo* is nursery-ish and Indo-Aryan-tinged. And 'good' is genuinely **loan-dominated** in modern spoken Gondi — *asal* (< Urdu *aslī*) and *chāṅglā* (< Marathi) are what people say. *cokkōṭ* is the most native option that a grammar attests, and it is what the row now carries; that is a defensible editorial choice, not a claim about frequency.
- **Two forms exist for several concepts across the continuum.** fire: *taḍmi* ~ *kis* (Adilabad). moon: *nelenj* (Adilabad) ~ *jaŋgo* (Betul/Yeotmal). dog: *nāy* (Betul, Maria) ~ *ney* (Adilabad, Koya). The row carries one; the other is real.
- **The Devanagari side of several cells is a transliteration**, not a sourced orthography — the underlying grammar (Subrahmanyam) is romanized. Directly attested script spellings: `एर / యేర్` (water), `लॊन / లొన` (house), `వెర్కడ్` (cat), `కిస్`/`తడ్మి` (fire), `పోడ్ద్` (sun), `नय्यु` (dog).
- The dual `Devanagari / Telugu` surface is **kept**, because it reflects how Gondi is actually written: Devanagari in the Madhya Pradesh north, Telugu in Telangana and Adilabad.

## Held — 2 cells
`love` (`प्रेम`, Sanskrit/Hindi) and `thanks` (`धन्यवाद`, Sanskrit/Hindi) remain. Neither researcher nor follow-up could source a Gondi word for either. They may well be genuine loans in modern speech — many languages in this atlas thank in a borrowed word — but nothing attests that for Gondi, so the cells were left rather than swapped for a guess. They are the first thing to revisit.
