# Review 519 — gloss fidelity of `head` and `new`

**Scope.** 454 rows, 908 cells, three slices, one question each: *does this cell
mean what the concept says it means?* Transcription, script and source questions
were explicitly out of scope and belong to rounds 520–522.

**Result.** 439 rows clean. One outright error, one unresolvable, thirteen notes.

The trap this round was built to catch — `new` filled with the word for *young*,
`head` filled with the word for *chief* or *hair* — came back **almost empty**.
Every IDS-covered row was checked against IDS's separate YOUNG entry (14-140)
and only one cell failed. That is a result about the filling pass rather than
about this round: the concept definitions were written before any cell was
filled and each one names what the word is NOT, and the fillers rejected roughly
one candidate in six on exactly those grounds before the reviewers ever saw
them.

## Changed

| row | word | was | now | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rap` Rapa Nui | new | ʻāpī | **hoʻou** | The young/new confusion, exactly as predicted. IDS lists `ʔāpī` under BOTH 14-130 NEW and 14-140 YOUNG. A Rapa Nui–Spanish dictionary gives `ho'ou = nuevo`, while `'apī` appears only in `vi'e 'apī` 'young woman' and `taŋa 'apī` 'young people'. ABVD and Walworth's Polynesian dataset both give `ho'ou`, and it is the reflex every sibling row uses (haw *hou*, sm *fou*, wls/rtm *foʻou*). |
| `acn` Achang | head | ni31kuaŋ31 | **nă31kuaŋ31** | The filler's own doubt, retired in the opposite direction from what it feared. It worried that `ni31` was the 'ear' morpheme — and it is: this row is built cell-for-cell from Sun Hongkai's TB database, whose Achang sun is `ni31 mɔ31` and ear `ni31 ʈʂʰua31`. The same database's head entry is `nă³¹kuaŋ³¹`. The candidate had simply copied the wrong vowel. |
| `zu` Zulu | new | omusha | **entsha** | The stem `-sha` is right; the concord was not. `omusha` is the class-1 form, and class 1 is the person class — `umuntu omusha` is 'a young person', which is precisely the reading this concept excludes. `entsha` is class 9 and modifies things (`imoto entsha` 'a new car'). |

## Open

**`enq` Enga head `ayamba` — suspect, left in place.** ABVD (Lang 1973) is the
only witness and lists three HEAD forms unranked and uncommented: ayámba ~
ayómba ~ maténá. ASJP has no Enga head entry at all, and ABVD's own Enga hair
entries (ítí, yomandí) share no morpheme with ayamba, so the internal
compound check that settled Makasae is unavailable here. **What would settle
it:** Lang's English index, or an Enga New Testament concordance.

## Decided, and recorded so it is not re-opened

**`el_grc` Ancient Greek new stays νέος.** καινός is the rival and looks
tempting, because LSJ's first gloss for νέος is 'young' — a sense this concept
excludes. It was kept anyway, on three grounds: the νεο- compounds are Greek's
regular way of saying 'newly made' (νεόδμητος, νεότευκτος); καινός's core value
is 'novel, unprecedented', which the concept *also* excludes; and IE-CoR selects
*néos* under a parameter worded "recently made, created or begun".

**French stays `nouveau` — all six rows.** The semantic argument runs the other
way and is worth writing down, because someone will make it again: *neuf* is
"qui vient d'être fait, qui n'a pas servi" (une voiture neuve), which is almost
word-for-word this concept's definition, while *nouveau* is "qui apparaît pour
la première fois", which shades into the excluded 'another, succeeding one'. And
*neuf* is the direct NOVUS reflex, cognate with the Spanish, Italian and
Portuguese cells beside it, where *nouveau* is from NOVELLUS.

It was kept regardless, because IE-CoR — the dataset this atlas already trusts
for this concept, and the one cited two paragraphs above to settle Greek —
selects **nouveau** for French, **novel** for Old French and **novê** for
Walloon: the NOVELLUS forms, deliberately, against Catalan *nou* and Romanian
*nou*. Six rows were one command from being rewritten on a reading of a
dictionary definition. Checking what the reference dataset actually says cost
one query.

## Notes carried forward

- **`wbp` / `wmt` are treated inconsistently.** Warlpiri new is `jalangu-warnu`
  'today-ASSOC', accepted because bowernpny and ASJP both give it and Warlpiri
  has no plainer adjective. Walmajarri's `calanycalany`, built on the same
  jalangu/jala root, was rejected. Walmajarri had a second reason (the form
  reduplicates that row's own *tongue* cell), but the two rows still sit on
  opposite sides of the same line.
- **`tay` new `geqas` is female register.** The lexeme is right — ASJP Squliq
  and the ILRDF glossary both lemmatise it — but ABVD tags the g-/ɣ- shape as
  FEMALE REGISTER in four Atayal doculects, with male-register `ʔiqas` alongside.
  The atlas has no convention for register-split lexicon.
- **`akb` `baru` vs `bbc` `imbaru`.** Two Batak rows write the same etymon
  differently. ABVD's Angkola and Mandailing lists give `baru`; ASJP
  BATAK_ANGKOLA gives `imbaroe`. Not a gloss error; a within-family consistency
  question for round 522.
- **`tkr` new `тезе`** is the Azerbaijani loan, making Tsakhur the only Lezgic
  row not using the inherited *cʼ- word. The native form's Cyrillic shape
  (Starling цIе-CLASS-ын, IDS "цедып") could not be verified.
- **`swi` head `ku3`** is genuinely glossed HEAD and is distinct from Sui hair
  `pjam¹`, but castrosui shows it in only 3 of 16 Sui doculects; 11, including
  standard Sandong, have `qam⁴`. A "major word only" question, not a gloss one.
- **`dta` head `oki`** — gloss sound (Dagur does use the Mongolic *eki root for
  the body part), but every primary attestation carries an initial x-; the
  vowel-initial shape rests on ASJP alone.
- **`bwi` head `hiwída`** — gloss confirmed; the Museu do Índio Baniwa-Koripako
  dictionary heads the entry `hewida (phewida)`, so the citation vowel may be e.
- **`mns` пуӈк = head + tooth is not a data error**, and the filler's reason for
  leaving it alone was wrong. It said Mansi tooth should be пуньк. IDS (4-270)
  and ASJP (param 43) both record `puŋk` for TOOTH as well: Northern Mansi
  merged two distinct Proto-Uralic etyma, *päŋe 'head' and *piŋi 'tooth'. Both
  cells are right. Recorded in tools/intra_row_dup_check.js.

## Retired doubts

Eleven of the fillers' own `uncertain` entries were positively resolved rather
than re-flagged. The pattern worth keeping: **the disambiguation was usually
already inside the source.** ABVD's Makasae hair is `daenasa` = `dae` 'head' +
`nasa`, which proves `dae` is the head word. ABVD's comments on Tuamotuan are
explicit — `uru` is "Head, hair", `upoko` is "Head of a man" — so the filler's
deviation from the first-listed variant was correct. Kusunda `ipi` is confirmed
by Watters' `ipi gidzaŋ` 'horn' (head-body), not by ABVD alone. Old French
`chief` is the inherited body part from Latin *caput*, not the 'chief' error it
looks like.
