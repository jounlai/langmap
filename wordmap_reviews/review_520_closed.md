# Review 520 — do the new cells match their row's conventions?

**Scope.** 454 rows, three slices. Not *is this the right word* (519) and not
*is it attested* (521) — only: is it **written** the way its 40–67 neighbours
are written? Script, orthography, IPA symbol inventory, stress, length, tone,
and whether the row splits surface from IPA or repeats one string in both.

**Result.** 418 rows clean, 19 cells corrected, 8 findings deliberately left
without a fix.

## The round caught a defect the previous round had just created

Its very first finding was `zu` new. Round 519 had changed that cell from
`omusha` to `entsha` an hour earlier, and written the IPA `enˈtʃʰa`. The Zulu
row marks ˈ on the penultimate syllable **without exception** — iˈkʰanda,
impuˈmulo, indodaˈkazi, iˈsondo, iˈkʰulu, kuˈɓili, iwaˈjini, inˈsimbi — so the
new cell was the row's only final-stressed one. That is the argument for
separating the rounds: a fix made on one axis is not checked on the others by
the person making it.

(Round 522 later moved that cell again, to the bare stem `sha`, for a third
reason. Three rounds, three different objections, one cell.)

**`pt_ao` was explicitly not touched.** Its `cabeça` is `kaˈbesɐ` and correct:

land on both.

## Three times the NEW cell was right and an EXISTING one was wrong

- **`te` Telugu wrote త two ways.** Telugu contrasts dental త with retroflex ట,
  so త is always /t̪/. head `t̪ala` and new `kot̪ːa` were on the correct side;
  `daughter`, `drink`, `eat`, `honey` and `white` had plain `t`. The row was
  normalised upward — five existing cells changed, none of the new ones.
- **`pms` Piedmontese.** new `neuv/nøw` is right; the row's `egg euv/ø` was
  missing the [w] that Piedmontese final `v` becomes. Two reviewers flagged it
  independently, one round apart. egg is now `øw`.
- **`lic` Hlai** writes ASCII `g` in the IPA — but so does the row's only other
  velar-stop cell, so the row is self-consistent and the debt is row-wide, not
  a defect in the new cell.

## Left without a fix, on purpose

A row that is inconsistent with itself cannot tell you which way to correct a
new cell, and guessing would make the inconsistency harder to see later. These
need one decision per row:

- **`bs` and `hr`** are split between plain ˈ (~30 cells) and Serbo-Croatian
  tone diacritics (~8). Both new Croatian cells landed on the minority side,
  and in `bs` the two new cells **disagree with each other** across the split:
  head `ˈɡlava`, new `nôv`.
- **`kac` Jingpho**: 35 of 54 cells carry a Chao letter, the new two carry
  none — but a third of the row is untoned, so there is no majority to follow
  and no source to invent one from.
- **`fud` Wallisian** uses two glottal marks (ʻ U+02BB and ASCII '), **`rar`
  Rarotongan** three (ʼ U+02BC, ʻ, ASCII '), **`tsg` Tausug** is split exactly
  4:4 between U+A78C ꞌ and ASCII '.
- **`ta` Tamil** renders word-final ஐ four different ways (ɛ, aj, aɪ, ai).
- **`kca` Khanty** is 5:4 between x and χ for ⟨х⟩.
- **`nio` Nganasan** does not mark palatalisation before ⟨е⟩ in four cells and
  does in others.
- **`cjm` Eastern Cham** mixes Cham script and Latin — a pre-existing state the
  new cells only join.

## Verified mechanically, not by eye

No new cell introduced a wrong script, a syllable-boundary dot, an ASCII colon
for length, or an ASCII `g` in a row that uses `ɡ`. The one dot that remains,
`och *s.tsʰi[n]`, is Baxter-Sagart preinitial notation and correct.
