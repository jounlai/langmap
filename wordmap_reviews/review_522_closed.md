# Review 522 — do the new cells cohere across related languages?

**Scope.** All 454 rows, grouped by family, three slices (A–I, J–S, T–Z plus
isolates, creoles, conlangs and proto-languages). The first three rounds each
looked at one cell at a time. This one looks across rows, because sound
correspondences between related languages are regular and an outlier is a
signal. It is the method that caught a mis-coded Gelao row in this atlas
earlier — not by examining the row, but by setting its forms beside a row
already on the map.

**Result.** 52 coherent groups recorded, 33 findings, 13 cells changed. It
found more real errors than any round except 523, and **none of them is visible
from inside the row.**

## Checked and kept — a loan is not automatically an error

- **`fa` جدید.** The only Iranian row taking the Arabic loan while every sister
  keeps *nawa- (tg нав, ku nû, ckb نوێ, ps نوی, yai nawa, srh neey, and
  historically xpr nawāg, ave nava). Kept: in Iranian Persian جدید is the
  ordinary attributive and نو has narrowed to 'brand-new'. The fa/tg split is
  real.
- **`tk` täze** (Persian *tāza*) — genuinely the everyday Turkmen word; *ýaňy*
  survives as 'just now'.
- **`itl` новой** — ASJP records exactly `ktxiŋ/noboy`, so the Russian loan is
  the source's, not the filler's.
- **`kjj` таза** — the Azerbaijani loan for 'new' has no competitor and stays,
  even though the same row's `head` loan did not.
- **`ksb` mpya** — flagged as suspiciously identical to Swahili. Kept: the
  Kishambaa New Testament has `mpya` eleven times and `mphya` never, and it is
  the regular class-9 form of the native stem `-hya`. Two Bantu languages
  arriving at the same string from the same root is not copying.

## Coherent, and worth recording

Polynesian `new` — all fourteen rows reflect PPn \*foʔou once Rapa Nui was
corrected in round 519. Turkic `new` — every row reflects \*yaŋɨ except the two
Persian loans above, both explained. Romance `new` — uniform NOVUS/NOVELLUS,
with the French branch's NOVELLUS choice confirmed against IE-CoR. Semitic,
Slavic, Germanic, Indo-Aryan, Mayan, Quechuan, Tupian, the English- and
French-based creoles: regular throughout. 52 groups in all. **A branch that
comes out regular is evidence the filling pass held together**, and is the
reason to trust the cells no reviewer had a comment on.

## Open

- **The proto-language rows have no `head` or `new` cells at all** — all 21 of
  them. The etymology paragraphs in `words/head.js` and `words/new.js` name
  \*baš, \*yaŋɨ, \*päŋe, \*wuδe, \*qulu, \*baqəRu and the rest, so the map says
  less than its own prose does. A coverage gap, not an error.
- **`zh_cq` head 头** where Chengdu and Kunming both have 脑壳 — and the
  Chongqing IPA `tʰəu˨˩` is character-for-character Liu 2007's *Chengdu* entry.
  Both the word choice and the reading need a Chongqing source.
- **`zh_xa` new ɕiŋ** — a velar rime for a 深臻 syllable in a dialect that keeps
  the distinction (Liu 2007: ɕiẽ²¹). Row-wide: 心 is written ɕiŋ too.
- **`tob`/`plg`/`moc`** carry a byte-identical `new`, which sits oddly beside
  their `head` cells, where the same three languages are allowed to differ.
