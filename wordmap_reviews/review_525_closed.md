**525 — coffee. Somali and Saho move off the bunna route, and my header was
wrong a second time in the same direction.**

  so  bun -> qaxwo, bunna -> qahwa (high). Qaamuuska Af-Soomaaliga, the
    standard monolingual dictionary, names three senses apart: bun is 1. the
    tree, 2. the fruit "from which qaxwo/kafee is made", 3. the fruit fried in
    oil or ghee; qaxwe is "made from roasted bun, drunk like tea". The cup is
    qaxwo. Somali Wikipedia titles its article Bun and Wikidata gives so=bun,
    so the loose usage is real — but only the dictionary distinguishes them,
    and this concept is the drink. Somalia is a large visible area and its
    colour turned on this.
  ssy buun -> qaxwa, bunna -> qahwa (medium). The premise I put in the header
    was simply false: I wrote that Saho "has since gone over to buun for the
    drink", and Vergari's current dictionary still carries the split verbatim —
    "buun nm coffee" beside "qaxwa nf coffee (beverage)".

The header now says what the evidence says: the highlands keep bunna (Amharic,
Tigrinya, Tigre, Oromo, Afar, Sidamo, Harari, Konso), and the Red Sea coast and
the Horn take qahwa for the drink while keeping bun for the bean — Blin and
Beja on Reinisch, Saho on Vergari, Somali on the Qaamuuska. bunna is 8 cells,
not 10.

That note has now been corrected twice, both times because I stated it more
strongly than the sources allowed. It is recorded in the file with both
corrections visible, because the pattern that emerged is better than the claim
I started with.

Verified rather than assumed: am ቡና (Kane), ti and tig ቡን — the Ginda Tigre
corpus has "I want to drink tea but my sister wants to drink coffee" = bun —
om buna (buna dhugaa), sid buna, har bun from Burton's Harari dialogues ("Dost
thou drink coffee? Bun tiseshákbi?"), kxc punitta from Ongaye 2013, with χolaa
confirmed as a separate lexeme for a coffee-LEAF brew. wal tukkiya holds as
`other`, corroborated off the crowd sources by a peer-reviewed Wolaita paper.
nds Koffie holds; wuu keeps citation tones, per the atlas's Mandarin convention.

A trap that looks like a flip and is not: Leslau's Harari qahwa is glossed
"drink made from the beans, leaves or husk of the coffee tree OR FROM THE TEA
LEAVES" — the generic word for a hot infusion. Harari bun qahwa is 'bean-brew',
not evidence of an Arabic route.

Afar is flagged as the next cell at risk: Parker & Hayward give bún/búna with
no split, but Reinisch's German index separates "Kaffee" from "Kaffeebohnen:
bun" and the OCR of the first word is unreadable.

**526 — cockcrow. 31 of ~40 source claims confirmed at the source, 8
unreachable and recorded as such, and one refuted.**

  ga  cuc-a-dúdal-dú -> coc-a-dúdal-dú. It is NOT an Ó Dónaill headword: FGB's
    list runs cucól · cu-cú · cud(r)amán, and a full-text search for dúdal
    returns no matches (the same search correctly returns the phrases block for
    coileach, so it works). What is printed is the New English–Irish
    Dictionary's interjection coc-a-dúdal-dú, with coc-.
  sw  kokoliko REMOVED. The claim was true but bare — Wiktionary, no dictionary,
    no corpus. That is the exact standard on which this word correctly left 33
    rows empty, including all 14 Arabic varieties, so keeping it would have
    meant applying one rule to Swahili and another to Arabic.

  The 28 English rows shared one IPA, following how woof and cuckoo were
  treated. That worked for those two because their vowels are FOOT and GOOSE,
  which these rows do not differentiate — but cock-a-doodle-doo opens on a LOT

  ɖɔɡ / dɔk / dɒɣ. en_us was carrying an ɒ found nowhere else in its row. Each
  row now takes the LOT vowel its own dog cell gives.

  On sr/hr the reviewer's verdict was to keep ˈ and leave the tonal diacritics
  alone: both rows are overwhelmingly ˈ-marked, so the cuckoo cell is the
  outlier, not this one.
