# Review 528 — closed

**Question:** Does each proto-language cell actually mean 'head' / 'new' in the source it cites?

**Scope:** the 33 proto cells added to `words/head.js` and `words/new.js`.
**Result:** 23 clean, 10 with notes, **0 outright wrong gloss** — but one wrongly-EMPTY cell, one
hold overturned, and one form that no source prints.

## The real defect is a cell that was left out

**`puaz` new — the recorded skip reason was false.** Handoff 95 said "all 2,703 Stubbs sets checked,
none glossed 'new'." Stubbs has a section headed **"NEW, YOUNG; NUEVO, JOVEN"**, and set 1523 reads
*"\*pïtuC / \*pïtuwa 'new' … [NUA: Num, Hp, Tb; SUA: Tep, Cah, CrC]"* — six branches across **both**
primary divisions. The skip is backwards: that is *broader* support than the `puaz` **head** cell
that was shipped, which Stubbs tags **[SUA]** only, noting that Northern Uto-Aztecan uses a
different etymon in every branch. The weaker cell was kept and the stronger dropped. **Filled** as
`*pɨtuwa`, ï normalised to ɨ, which is this row's own majority spelling.

## A hold overturned by its own source

**`psem` new** was held back on SHAPE — an unvocalised root where every other psem cell is a
vocalised stem. The source answers that objection directly: StarLing prints **`*ḥad(i)t̠-`,
meaning "new"** — a vocalised adjectival stem, with adjectival reflexes in Akkadian ešš-, Ugaritic
ḥdt̠, Hebrew ḥādāš, Geʕez ḥäddis, Mehri ḥeydīn. **Filled** as `*ḥad(i)ṯ-`, keeping the optional
vowel in parentheses the way `pmay` new already keeps `*ʔa(a)kʼ`.

**`pafa` new stays out** — HSED glosses `*may/ʔ-` as **"be new"**, a stative verb rather than an
adjective, on Egyptian and Chadic only. The hold was right.

## A form no source prints

**`p_ine` head `*ḱr̥rēh₂`** — exact-string search returns zero hits anywhere. The root is
`*ḱerh₂-`, glossed **"head, top" AND "horn"** in the same entry, and the 'head' word is the s-stem
derived from it. **Corrected to `*ḱérh₂os`**, and the prose with it — which now also says that the
root covers 'horn', since that is how Latin cornū belongs to the same entry.

## Both declared loans confirmed, one stronger and one weaker than declared

- **`ptai` head `*truə` — worse than the note said.** Pittayaporn 2009, Appendix B, note 2:
  *"cf. 頭 tóu 'head' … **This etymon is not found in NT, and might in fact be a post-PT
  loanword.**"* He does not merely compare 頭; he doubts it is Proto-Tai at all. (His other 'head'
  entry, `*krawC`, is disqualified by his own note 1 as really 'hair knot', so the pick was right.)
- **`p_hmx` new `*tshji̯əŋ` — partly refuted.** Ratliff's gloss is clean and at PHM level, but 清
  sits in her *comparanda* column, whose header reads *"not all Chinese comparanda listed are
  necessarily cognates or related forms."* The 新 comparison was an editorial addition, not hers.

**Neither caveat was visible to a reader** — none of the 33 cells carried an inline comment, unlike
`hundred.js` where `pura: ["*śata", …], // borrowed from Indo-Iranian`. Comments added.

## Level, not meaning

`pura` new is UEW #1173 at level **FU** (no Samoyedic) against `päŋe` at level **U** — disclosed in
the prose but not at the cell. `p_sit` is Matisoff **PTB**, not Proto-Sino-Tibetan — but that is a
row-wide convention (`*s-nəw`, `*r-miŋ`, `*g-sum` are already PTB), not a new defect. `p_jpn` head
`*kasira` comes from a mainland-only database: no Ryukyuan reflex means 'head' (Okinawan *chiburu*;
Ryukyuan かしら is 'chief'). `p_dra` `*talay` is DEDR 3103 "head, hair, top", an entry that also
carries 'leadership', 'chief', 'beginning'. `pban` new `*-pɪ́à` is BLR3 status **DER**, derived from
2491 `*-pɪ́` 'be burnt; be hot; be ripe'.

A useful pattern fell out of the Austronesian lookups: **the higher proto-level carries the tighter
gloss** — PAN `*quluh` is 'head' while PMP `*qulu` is 'head; top part; leader, chief; headwaters;
prow of a boat; first-born'. Citing PAN was the right instinct.

## Three skip reasons were right but recorded wrongly

`pmay` head holds, but the rejected headword is **GLL `*jo7l` (Greater Lowland)**, not "an Eastern
Mayan form". `p_tun` new holds, but the best rejected candidate is `*ime-` **'fresh, new'** in five
languages, not `*xir-`. `p_ryu` head is a ninth gap with **no recorded reason at all** — the skip is
right (Wiktionary's PR `*atama` has only Okinawan and a Tarama poetic form, both Japanese-source)
but it was undocumented.
