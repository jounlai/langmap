# Review 679: stress marks from Wiktionary in half-marked rows

2026-11-09. Stress-mark policy (owner 2026-07-21): polysyllabic single words carry ˈ, monosyllables and phrases do not; only English had been normalised. Rows where 40% or more of the polysyllabic single-word cells already carry ˈ (and not all) are rows whose own convention is to mark stress. In those rows, a polysyllabic cell without ˈ got the mark only when its surface is a Wiktionary headword whose IPA marks stress, with the same syllable count and no non-syllabic vowels; the mark goes before the onset of the same syllable (~/langmap-work/stress1109/plan.py).

216 cells, most in Indonesian 33, Malay 26, Persian 24, Bashkir 18, Kazakh 12, Slovak 10, Danish 8, Nheengatu 8. Cells with no Wiktionary IPA (1,830) or no stress in it (165) are left; rows that mark under 40% (French, tonal and pitch-accent rows among them) were not touched.

Addendum: fixed initial-stress rows. Where stress always falls on the first syllable (Finnish, Hungarian, Czech, Slovak, Icelandic, Karelian, Livvi, Kven, the Sámi languages, Mansi, Khanty), every unmarked polysyllabic single word got ˈ at the start: 310 cells, most in Lule 72, Skolt 68 and Inari Sámi 63, Mansi 41, Khanty 32. Rows with loanword exceptions (Faroese, Latvian, Estonian, Veps, the Sorbian languages, Võro, Latgalian) were left, as were Silesian and Kashubian (penultimate).

Addendum 2: fixed penultimate-stress rows. Aymara (76), Cusco Quechua (52), Quechua (11) and Swahili (3) got ˈ on the second-last syllable, before its onset consonant (one consonant; affricates and Swahili prenasalised stops count as one; a word-initial cluster stays together, as in Grisya ˈɡɾisja). Swahili buluu is three syllables, buˈluː. Aymara café and celular, Spanish loans stressed on the last syllable, were left unmarked. Esperanto, Polish, Silesian and Kashubian were already marked; Welsh, Breton and Cornish (diphthongs and exceptions) and the Polynesian rows (no marks at all, moraic stress) were not touched.

Addendum 3: Nahuatl rows (Classical 61, Western Huasteca 46, Huasteca 44, Central Huasteca 42, Nawat 27), regular penultimate stress, 220 cells; the absolutive -tl/-tli counts as its own syllable only when it carries a vowel (temaˈlakatɬ, teˈpostɬi). Eleven cells with a vowel sequence (hiatus or diphthong unclear) were left. Zulu, Xhosa and Chichewa were tried and reverted: they are tone languages, and the policy keeps ˈ off tonal rows; their existing marks were not touched.

Addendum 4: Turkic final stress. In the Turkic rows the existing marks sit on the last syllable except in loans, compounds and country names (coffee, potato, jellyfish, Japan, Germany ...). 370 unmarked cells of native core words got ˈ on the last syllable, in 15 rows (Yakut 34, Kumyk 32, Khakas 31, Nogai 31, Uyghur 29, Karachay-Balkar 27, Tatar 26 ...). Partial words and every concept likely to be a loan or compound (country names, days, drinks, crops, animals often borrowed, kinship claim words) were skipped, as were cells with a vowel sequence. Turkish, Azerbaijani, Kyrgyz, Bashkir and Gagauz were already almost fully marked; Chuvash (stress on the last full vowel) and Kalmyk (Mongolic, initial) were not touched.

Addendum 5: the Wiktionary-sourced pass extended to rows that mark 10-40% of their cells (tonal, pitch-accent and French rows excluded): 314 cells, most in Tagalog-script tsg 43, Gujarati 28, Pitjantjatjara 27, Sranan 25, Meadow Mari 24, Udmurt 21. Rows with no marks at all (Māori, Hawaiian, Bengali, ...) were left as they are, so a partial source would not make a consistent row inconsistent.
